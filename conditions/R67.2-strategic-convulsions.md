# R67.2 · Strategic convulsions

## 1. Diagnostic Criteria

A. Two (or more) strategic moves within a twelve-month period, each of which meets all of the following:

1. The move is announced as central to the patient’s future.
2. The move is inconsistent with the patient’s prior stated strategy, or with another move in the same period.
3. The move was not preceded by evidence that customers wanted it.

B. The moves include at least two of the following:

1. A rebrand, or a renamed product line, that adds *AI* or *agent* to the name.
2. An assistant added to the side of every screen in the existing product.
3. Acquisition of a challenger at a price the patient’s prior statements (see [R67.1](R67.1-copium-magnificum.md)) cannot explain.
4. Restriction of customers’ access to their own data, through API pricing, rate limits, or terms that forbid use by agents.
5. A reorganization announced together with a new strategy and reversed within the following year.
6. A price increase for a capability the patient also describes as included or “at no additional cost.”
7. A developer platform announced in a category where the patient previously restricted third-party access.

C. The clinician cannot reconstruct a coherent plan that connects the moves from the patient’s public materials.

D. The moves are not better explained by [R67.3 · Hail Mary](R67.3-hail-mary.md), in which resources are committed to a single outcome.

*Specify* current severity, by the number of qualifying moves in the past twelve months:

- **Mild:** 2 moves.
- **Moderate:** 3–4 moves.
- **Severe:** 5 or more moves.

*Specify* if:

- **With contradictory movements:** Two moves in the same period work against each other, such as opening a developer platform and restricting API access in the same quarter.
- **With enclosure:** At least one move restricts customers’ access to their own data (Criterion B4).

## 2. Recording Procedures

List each move with its announcement date, its stated rationale and its source. The clinician’s reconstruction of the actual rationale is an inference and is recorded as such. Where two moves contradict each other, record both and apply the specifier.

## 3. Diagnostic Features

Senseless strategic moves made as the patient flounders to preserve its position and survive.

Each move, taken alone, usually resembles an ordinary business maneuver: a rebrand, an acquisition, a reorganization, a pricing change. This is why the condition is underdiagnosed. The diagnosis rests on the pattern across moves, not on any single one. The clinician who recognizes each convulsion as a familiar maneuver should resist the inclination to prescribe another.

## 4. Associated Features

Press releases in which the word *bold* appears more than once. An executive departure within ninety days of a move. A reduction in force announced in the same post as an “AI-first” strategy. Increasing use of the phrase *doubling down*.

## 5. Prevalence

Not known. Clinicians report that it is rising.

## 6. Development and Course

Onset typically follows the point at which [R67.1 · Copium magnificum](R67.1-copium-magnificum.md) stops working on investors. The course is paroxysmal. Convulsions cluster around earnings, the annual customer conference and challenger launches, and the interval between them shortens as the condition progresses.

## 7. Risk and Prognostic Factors

**Environmental.** A chief executive in the first eighteen months of tenure. An activist investor. A challenger demonstration that was widely shared.

**Course modifiers.** Prognosis improves when the patient stops announcing moves before it has decided what they are for.

## 8. Differential Diagnosis

**Pivot.** A change of strategy that is coherent, sustained and preceded by evidence. A pivot is made once.

**[R67.3 · Hail Mary](R67.3-hail-mary.md).** A Hail Mary commits resources to one outcome. Convulsions spread them across several, often in opposing directions.

**[R67.4 · Delirium](R67.4-delirium.md).** Strategic convulsions are one way delirium may show up in the patient’s decisions. Convulsions describe behavior; delirium describes reasoning. Both may be diagnosed when both are present.

**Ordinary portfolio management.** Periodic acquisitions and divestitures consistent with a stated strategy. Not a disorder.

## 9. Comorbidity

[R67.1 · Copium magnificum](R67.1-copium-magnificum.md) is present in nearly all cases. [R67.4 · Delirium](R67.4-delirium.md) is common in severe presentations.
