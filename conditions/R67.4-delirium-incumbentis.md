# R67.4 · Delirium incumbentis

## 1. Diagnostic Criteria

A. A disturbance in attention (reduced ability to direct, focus, sustain and shift attention) and awareness (reduced orientation to the market).

B. The disturbance develops over a short period, usually one or two quarters, represents a change from the patient’s baseline reasoning, and tends to fluctuate in severity.

C. An additional disturbance in cognition, such as:

1. **Contradiction.** The patient holds two positions in the same period that cannot both be true.
2. **Disorientation to time.** The patient describes the current market as it was three or more years earlier.
3. **Misidentification.** The patient names the wrong competitor, or does not recognize the challenger as a competitor.
4. **Perceptual disturbance.** The patient reports demand that the clinician cannot locate.

D. The disturbances in Criteria A and C are not better explained by a coherent strategy the clinician has failed to understand.

E. There is evidence from the history or the record that the disturbance is a consequence of [R67.1 · Copium maximum](R67.1-copium-maximum.md), of its withdrawal, or of multiple causes.

*Specify* whether:

- **Copium intoxication delirium:** Follows prolonged or excessive consumption of copium.
- **Copium withdrawal delirium:** Follows the sudden loss of a reassuring account, as after a failed [R67.3 · Ave Maria](R67.3-ave-maria.md) or a lost anchor customer.
- **Delirium due to multiple etiologies.**

*Specify* if:

- **Acute:** Lasting days, often a single earnings call and its aftermath.
- **Persistent:** Lasting weeks or months.

*Specify* if:

- **Hyperactive:** Elevated rate of announcements; see [R67.2 · Convulsio strategica](R67.2-convulsio-strategica.md).
- **Hypoactive:** No announcements. Executives withdraw from public channels.
- **Mixed level of activity.**

## 2. Recording Procedures

Record two or more statements or decisions from the same period that cannot both be true, with dates and sources. A single implausible statement is not sufficient for diagnosis.

## 3. Diagnostic Features

An acute loss of coherent reasoning.

May follow prolonged or excessive consumption of copium.

A common presentation: the patient states, in one quarter, that AI will automate the work its customers do, and that its customers will continue to pay per seat for the people who did it.

## 4. Associated Features

Rapid turnover of vocabulary, with the patient adopting the challenger’s terms within weeks of their first use. Public statements timestamped between 2 and 4 a.m. Disagreement between what the chief executive says and what the chief financial officer says on the same call.

## 5. Prevalence

Increases with the duration of [R67.1](R67.1-copium-maximum.md). Rare in patients without a history of copium.

## 6. Development and Course

The course fluctuates. Lucid intervals are common, often in the days after a board meeting. In human patients delirium is treated as an emergency. In incumbents it is usually first noticed by customers.

## 7. Risk and Prognostic Factors

**Environmental.** Prolonged or excessive copium. Sudden withdrawal of copium. Executive turnover, which removes the people who remembered the prior strategy.

**Course modifiers.** Acute presentations often resolve once the patient stops speaking in public. Persistent presentations have a poor prognosis.

## 8. Differential Diagnosis

**[R67.1 · Copium maximum](R67.1-copium-maximum.md).** Coherent reasoning on comfortable premises. In delirium the reasoning itself fails.

**[R67.2 · Convulsio strategica](R67.2-convulsio-strategica.md).** Strategic convulsions are one way delirium may show up in the patient’s decisions. Convulsions describe behavior; delirium describes reasoning.

**Audience-specific messaging.** Different statements made deliberately to different audiences. Distinguish by whether the patient knows which audience it is addressing.

## 9. Comorbidity

[R67.1](R67.1-copium-maximum.md) in nearly all cases. [R67.2](R67.2-convulsio-strategica.md) in hyperactive presentations.
