# F67.3 · Ave Maria

*Common name:* Hail Mary

## 1. Diagnostic Criteria

A. The patient commits a majority of its remaining discretionary resources (cash, engineering capacity or executive attention) to a single outcome.

B. The outcome has a low probability of arriving within the patient’s remaining runway, by the patient’s own estimates or by base rates the clinician can cite.

C. The patient has no plan for the case in which the outcome does not arrive, or has one it declines to discuss.

D. The patient describes the commitment in terms of conviction rather than odds.

E. The commitment is not better explained by [F67.2 · Convulsio strategica](F67.2-convulsio-strategica.md) or by a financed bet that the patient can survive losing.

*Coding note:* The code depends on type.

- **F67.30 Commercial type:** The outcome is a single customer or contract.
- **F67.31 Corporate type:** The outcome is a single acquirer, merger or strategic partner.
- **F67.32 Product type:** The outcome is a single launch.
- **F67.33 Financial type:** The outcome is a single funding round, loan or covenant waiver.

*Specify* runway at diagnosis, in weeks.

## 2. Recording Procedures

Record the outcome the patient depends on, the runway at the time of commitment, and the date by which the outcome must arrive. Record the type and the runway as specifiers, e.g. *F67.30 Ave Maria, commercial type, runway 6 weeks*.

## 3. Diagnostic Features

The patient commits its remaining resources to a single unlikely outcome. Survival now depends on that outcome arriving in time.

The distinction from an ordinary bet is survival. A bet can be lost. A Hail Mary cannot be lost without losing the patient.

## 4. Associated Features

Secrecy about the object of the commitment, often justified as discretion. Personal involvement of the chief executive in a single deal. Board meetings scheduled closer together. Increased use of the word *transformational*.

> **Case note.** Patient reports six weeks of cash and one prospective enterprise customer. Asked how long procurement usually takes, patient requests that the customer’s name be withheld until the deal closes.

## 5. Prevalence

Common in the terminal phase of illness. Rare earlier, since a patient with resources to spare is not yet compelled to concentrate them.

## 6. Development and Course

Onset is usually late, after [F67.2 · Convulsio strategica](F67.2-convulsio-strategica.md) have consumed the resources that would have allowed a hedge. The outcome is binary and observable, which makes this the most reliably diagnosed condition in the chapter. The course ends at the deadline. Either the outcome arrives, and the patient enters remission, or it does not.

## 7. Risk and Prognostic Factors

**Environmental.** Short runway. A single customer that accounts for a large share of revenue. A prospective acquirer that has already said no once.

**Course modifiers.** Prognosis is poor. Remission after a successful Hail Mary is commonly followed by recurrence, because the conditions that required it remain.

## 8. Differential Diagnosis

**Focus.** Concentration of resources with enough runway to survive failure. Not a disorder.

**[F67.2 · Convulsio strategica](F67.2-convulsio-strategica.md).** Convulsions spread resources across many moves; a Hail Mary concentrates them on one.

**Sale process.** A sale run as a process with several bidders is not a Hail Mary. A sale that depends on one named buyer may be.

## 9. Comorbidity

[F67.1 · Copium maximum](F67.1-copium-maximum.md) is usually present and concerns the probability of the outcome.
