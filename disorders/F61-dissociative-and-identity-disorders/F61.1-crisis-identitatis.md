# F61.1 · Crisis identitatis

*Common name:* Identity crisis

## 1. Diagnostic Criteria

A. A marked and persistent instability in the patient’s account of what it is, as indicated by two (or more) of the following:

1. Describes itself, or its next product, with the name of another category, typically one in which a challenger is growing (“an AI company,” “an operating system for work,” “a development environment”).
2. States that it is already equipped for the new identity, citing what it has (data, users, a position “at the center of the workflow”) rather than what it has built.
3. Defines the new identity to absorb the categories on either side of the original one (“everything in one place,” “from idea to production”).
4. Describes its original product as “just the beginning” or “only the start.”
5. Rewrites its mission statement, homepage or category description more than once in twelve months.

B. Onset follows the arrival of a challenger, or of a technology, that makes the patient’s original category look smaller.

C. The new identity is stated in public before it appears in the patient’s product, hiring or pricing.

D. The instability is not better explained by a coherent expansion supported by evidence of customer demand, or by [F62.1 · Convulsio strategica](../F62-disruption-and-impulse-control-disorders/F62.1-convulsio-strategica.md), in which the instability appears in moves rather than in statements of identity.

*Specify* if:

- **With grandiosity:** The new identity is larger than the original one and contains it.
- **With borrowed identity:** The new identity is the category of the challenger that precipitated the crisis.
- **With good or fair insight, poor insight, absent insight** or **insight unknown,** as for [F60.1](../F60-copium-spectrum-and-other-delusional-disorders/F60.1-copium-maximum.md).

## 2. Recording Procedures

Record each statement of identity with its date, setting and source, and the category it names. Record what the patient sold at the time of the statement. The gap between the two is the finding. Paraphrase; do not quote.

A single statement may meet Criterion A when it meets two of its items, as a statement that the patient is equipped for a larger identity that absorbs its neighbors meets A2 and A3.

## 3. Diagnostic Features

The essential feature is that the patient no longer accepts the category its customers buy it for. Asked what it is, the patient answers with what it intends to become, and the answer is usually the name of whatever is growing fastest nearby.

The crisis is rarely experienced as a crisis. Patients describe it as ambition, and observers frequently share the description. The clinician distinguishes ambition from crisis by sequence: ambition builds the new product and then names it; crisis names it first.

## 4. Associated Features

A new homepage headline with no new product beneath it. The word *platform*. Diagrams in which the patient’s logo appears at the center of a circle of other categories. A founder letter titled with the word *next*.

## 5. Prevalence

Common, and highest in categories adjacent to a fast-growing one. Clinicians report that nearly every software category has, at some point in the past two years, described itself as the place where work now happens.

## 6. Development and Course

Onset is typically sudden and public, often a single post or keynote. The course is variable. In some patients the stated identity is later built, and the episode is reclassified as a pivot in retrospect. In others it is restated at each conference with a different category name.

Patients that were recently challengers themselves appear to be at elevated risk. Having displaced an incumbent once, the patient believes it can enter any adjacent category the same way.

## 7. Risk and Prognostic Factors

**Temperamental.** A founder-led patient. A history of having been the challenger.

**Environmental.** A challenger category that is growing faster than the patient’s own. Users who are vocal in public and reward ambition with approval.

**Course modifiers.** Prognosis improves when the patient ships something in the new category before naming itself after it.

## 8. Differential Diagnosis

**Platform expansion.** A patient that builds adjacent products in response to customer demand, and describes them after they ship. Not a disorder.

**[F62.1 · Convulsio strategica](../F62-disruption-and-impulse-control-disorders/F62.1-convulsio-strategica.md).** Convulsions are moves. Identity crisis is a statement about what the patient is. The two often co-occur, since a convulsion is frequently the first attempt to act out a new identity.

**[F60.1 · Copium maximum](../F60-copium-spectrum-and-other-delusional-disorders/F60.1-copium-maximum.md).** Copium reassures the patient that it does not need to change. Identity crisis announces that it already has.

## 9. Comorbidity

Commonly co-occurs with [F60.1 · Copium maximum](../F60-copium-spectrum-and-other-delusional-disorders/F60.1-copium-maximum.md), especially Criterion A2, the claim to be already equipped. Frequently precedes [F62.1 · Convulsio strategica](../F62-disruption-and-impulse-control-disorders/F62.1-convulsio-strategica.md).
