# F67.1 · Copium maximum

## 1. Diagnostic Criteria

A. A marked inclination to consume excessive amounts of copium, lasting at least two consecutive quarters, as indicated by three (or more) of the following observable signs:

1. Asserts that the patient’s data, installed base or customer relationships constitute a moat, without describing how the moat prevents a customer from leaving.
2. Describes a new category of competitor as “a feature, not a product.”
3. Cites retention, logo count or customer satisfaction as evidence about the future rather than the past.
4. States that enterprise customers will not adopt the new approach for reasons of security, compliance or governance, and does not revisit the statement as those customers adopt it.
5. Reports that the patient “has been doing AI for years.”
6. Attributes slowing growth to macroeconomic conditions in a quarter in which the category’s challengers grew.

B. Consumption increases in frequency or dose as contrary evidence accumulates.

C. The consumption causes clinically significant impairment in strategic, product or capital-allocation functioning.

D. The statements are not better explained by a disclosure obligation, a renewal in progress, or a deliberate effort to steady employees (see [§8](#8-differential-diagnosis)).

*Coding note:* The code depends on current severity.

- **F67.10 Mild:** Statements are confined to earnings calls and investor materials.
- **F67.11 Moderate:** Statements appear in all-hands meetings and are repeated by middle management.
- **F67.12 Severe:** The patient makes product or capital decisions on the basis of the statements.

*Specify* if:

- **With good or fair insight:** The patient recognizes, in private, that the statements may not be true.
- **With poor insight:** The patient thinks the statements are probably true.
- **With absent insight:** The patient is completely convinced the statements are true.
- **Insight unknown:** See [§2](#2-recording-procedures).

## 2. Recording Procedures

Record what the patient says has reassured them. A public statement alone leaves open whether its author believes it.

The insight specifier therefore cannot be assigned from public statements alone. Record *insight unknown* unless private statements, internal documents or the patient’s own decisions corroborate a level of insight. A patient who reassures investors while quietly cutting the affected product line has good insight. A patient who reassures investors and then hires against the statement has absent insight.

Record each qualifying statement with its date, setting (earnings call, keynote, post, interview) and source. Paraphrase; do not quote.

## 3. Diagnostic Features

The essential feature is a marked inclination to consume copium in large quantities. Copium, for the purposes of this manual, is an account of the patient’s position that the patient adopts because it reassures, not because the evidence supports it.

Doses are typically taken at earnings calls, at the annual customer conference and in replies to posts by challengers. Most patients also produce copium for others, chiefly investors and employees. The clinician should distinguish production from consumption. Production alone is a communications strategy. Consumption is the disorder.

## 4. Associated Features

Increased use of the words *durable*, *trusted* and *platform*. Frequent references to the size of the installed base, usually in the first five minutes of a presentation. A competitive landscape slide on which the patient occupies the upper-right quadrant and the challenger is absent or appears as a small logo near the axis.

In founder-led patients, public posts may be made between midnight and 4 a.m.

## 5. Prevalence

Prevalence among incumbents in categories with an active AI-native challenger is not known, because patients do not report the disorder. Clinicians in the field consider it the modal presentation. Prevalence is lower in categories where no challenger has yet raised a Series B.

## 6. Development and Course

Onset usually follows the first credible demonstration of a challenger product, often within one news cycle. The early course is episodic, with doses concentrated around earnings. Untreated, the course is chronic.

Prolonged or excessive consumption may lead to [F67.4 · Delirium incumbentis](F67.4-delirium-incumbentis.md).

## 7. Risk and Prognostic Factors

**Temperamental.** A history of having been the challenger. Patients who displaced a prior incumbent are more confident that the pattern will not repeat.

**Environmental.** High gross margins. Multiyear contracts, which delay the arrival of evidence. A board composed largely of people who were present at the founding.

**Course modifiers.** Prognosis improves when the patient begins to measure what its customers do with a challenger, rather than what they say about it.

## 8. Differential Diagnosis

**Justified confidence.** The patient’s statements are supported by evidence the clinician can observe, such as retention among the customers most exposed to the challenger. Not a disorder.

**Disclosure-constrained speech.** A public company may be unable to say otherwise. Distinguish by private statements and by how the patient allocates capital.

**Renewal posture.** Reassurance offered to a single customer during a renewal. Transient; resolves at signature.

**[F67.4 · Delirium incumbentis](F67.4-delirium-incumbentis.md).** In copium maximum the patient’s reasoning is coherent, but its premises are selected for comfort. In delirium the reasoning itself fails.

## 9. Comorbidity

Commonly co-occurs with [F67.2 · Convulsio strategica](F67.2-convulsio-strategica.md). A convulsion is often the patient’s attempt to make a prior reassuring statement true after the fact.
